Water vs Alcohol vs Dual Extraction, Explained

Published 2026-09-04 · Last reviewed 2026-10-19

Two Reishi extracts can start from the exact same mushroom and end up with different compounds in the bottle, purely because of how they were made. Extraction method is one of the least advertised and most consequential variables in a mushroom supplement, so it is worth understanding in plain terms. There are three approaches you will run into, and each one captures a different part of the mushroom.

Why method changes the contents

Mushrooms contain two broad families of compounds relevant here, and they dissolve in different things.

  • Beta-glucans and other cell-wall polysaccharides are water-associated. Hot water pulls them out.
  • Triterpenes, the bitter compounds concentrated in Reishi, are fat-associated. Water largely leaves them behind. Alcohol pulls them out.

That single fact drives the entire extraction conversation. Whatever solvent a brand uses determines which of these two families makes it into your bottle.

Water extraction

A hot water extract is the traditional approach and the most common. It is good at capturing beta-glucans, which is why water-only extracts can post respectable beta-glucan numbers.

Its limit is what it misses. A water-only Reishi extract will contain little to none of the triterpenes, because water does not dissolve them well. For a mushroom like Lion's Mane, where the compounds of interest are more water-friendly, a water extract is less of a compromise. For Reishi, water alone leaves a whole compound family on the table.

Alcohol extraction

An alcohol extraction does the opposite. It is effective at pulling out the fat-associated triterpenes but poor at capturing the water-associated beta-glucans. On its own, an alcohol-only extract is as incomplete as a water-only one, just incomplete in the other direction.

This is why alcohol extraction is rarely the whole story for a quality product. It is usually one half of a two-part process.

Dual extraction

Dual extraction runs both steps, a hot water extraction and an alcohol extraction, and combines the results. The point is to capture both families: the beta-glucans from the water step and the triterpenes from the alcohol step. For Reishi especially, this is the method that matches the marketing when a brand claims a full-spectrum or whole-compound benefit.

Dual extraction is more work and more cost, which is exactly why a brand that does it tends to say so, and a brand that does not tends to stay quiet about method.

One note that matters for some buyers: dual extraction uses alcohol during processing, but a well-made finished extract can still be alcohol-free in the bottle if the alcohol is removed after it has done its job. Method during production and what remains in the final liquid are two separate questions worth asking.

How to read the method off a label

Method is often unstated, so you infer it from the compounds reported:

  • A Reishi product reporting beta-glucan but no triterpenes at all is almost certainly water-only.
  • A Reishi product claiming full-spectrum or triterpene content, and reporting both beta-glucan and triterpenes, points to dual extraction.
  • A product that names its method outright, water, alcohol, or dual, is doing you a favor and is usually confident in the answer.

The takeaway is simple. For Lion's Mane, a good water extraction covers most of what you are after. For Reishi, if the label promises the full range of benefits but the numbers show only water-soluble compounds, the method and the promise do not agree, and the method is telling the truth.
