Lion's Mane and Reishi extracts, compared on published numbers

Twenty-six extracts, judged on the four things a shopper can actually check: the part of the mushroom used, the extraction method, the active-compound figure the brand publishes, and whether you can open a certificate of analysis. Every figure comes from the brand's own page or COA. A number nobody publishes is recorded as not published, never estimated.

What is in scope. Concentrated extracts only: hot water, alcohol, or both. Milled mycelium grown on grain, plain mushroom powder, blends, coffees and gummies are not extracts, whatever the label says, so they are listed separately with the brand's own wording and the reason, rather than scored against products that are.

Every figure is quoted from the brand's own product page or its own certificate of analysis. Where a number is not published, the cell says so instead of carrying an estimate. Five stars means all four of: fruiting body, a stated extraction method, a published active-compound figure, and a COA you can open in one click. Each one missing drops a star. A polysaccharide total is not a beta-glucan figure and does not earn that point, which is why two brands advertising 40% and 20% sit near the bottom.
