Lion's Mane and Reishi extracts, ranked

Twenty concentrated extracts scored against six weighted criteria, every figure checked against the brand's own product page or its own certificate of analysis, and fourteen brands found to be selling something that is not an extract at all.

What is in scope. Concentrated extracts only: hot water, alcohol, or both. A product is ranked only where the brand’s own page calls it an extract or states an extraction method or ratio. Mycelium grown on grain, plain mushroom powder, blends, coffees and gummies are not extracts, whatever the label says, and are listed separately with the brand’s own wording and the reason.

How the scores are built

Six weighted criteria, applied identically to every product in both tables.

CriterionWeightWhat we look for
Extraction depth and compound completeness25%Is this a concentrated extract, and how completely does the maker disclose what is in it? Full compound mapping scores highest, a single standardized percentage scores in the middle, and an undisclosed extraction scores lowest.
Bioavailability and delivery format20%How is the product built to be absorbed? Liquid sublingual delivery scores highest, capsules and powders in the middle, and anything diluted with its growing medium lowest.
Third-party testing and COA access20%Is there current, accessible, independent testing? A per-lot certificate you can open in one click scores highest, a COA on request in the middle, a testing claim with no document lowest.
Source material15%Fruiting body against mycelium on grain, the species named in full, and organic sourcing.
Label and sourcing transparency10%Extraction method and ratio stated, no proprietary blends hiding the quantities, clear origin.
Value against verified quality10%Price measured against what is actually proven to be inside, not the lowest sticker price.

Sold as extracts, but not extracts

These are the products a shopper most often puts beside the tables above. Each is quoted from the brand’s own page, because in almost every case the brand says it plainly and the marketing says something else.

  • Host Defense — Mycelium grown on fermented brown rice, milled rather than extracted. Their own FAQ: “we don’t isolate or concentrate any particular compounds in our powdered products.” The “over 55% polysaccharides” guarantee is a polysaccharide total on a rice matrix, not a beta-glucan figure.
  • Om Mushrooms — Mycelial biomass and fruit body cultured on whole oats, with the oats included in the 2 g serving. Their own FAQ: “No. We don’t use hot water or alcohol extraction.”
  • Genius Mushrooms — Mycelium on oats, and extract-free by design: “Made with 100% extract-free, 100% organic mushrooms.”
  • Solaray — Fermented mycelium cultured on whole oats or brown rice. No extraction method and no ratio published.
  • Nutricost — Labelled mushroom powder, not an extract. No method, no ratio, no actives figure.
  • NOW Foods — The Lion’s Mane is sold as “extract powder” with no method, no ratio, no actives figure and no COA, so there is nothing to score; the Rei-Shi product is a reishi and shiitake blend.
  • Swanson — Full Spectrum is “whole reishi mushroom rather than a concentrated extract” in their own words. The standardized products publish a polysaccharide total, which includes grain starch, and no method.
  • Micro Ingredients — “Freeze-dried extract” is a drying step, not an extraction. The Reishi powder claims a 100:1 ratio and does not state what part of the mushroom is in it.
  • Terrasoul Superfoods — A genuine 4:1 water extract from log-grown fruiting bodies, but no actives figure and no COA, so there is nothing to score it on.
  • Double Wood — The Lion’s Mane is not an extract in the brand’s own words. The Reishi is a 4:1 extract whose published COA assays identity, metals and microbials, but no actives.
  • Toniiq — Publishes the best single document in the survey, a one-click COA with a Megazyme beta-glucan assay, but the product page calls it whole mushroom powder and states no extraction method.
  • Sun Potion — Their own spec table: “Full-spectrum mushroom powder (not an extract)”, despite publishing 52.2% beta-glucans on a recent Lion’s Mane lot.
  • Four Sigmatic — No single-species Lion’s Mane or Reishi extract. Every mushroom product is a blend, coffee, cocoa or elixir packet.
  • Gummies, coffees and drink mixes — Excluded as a format across every brand in the survey.

How each cell was checked

One pass, against each brand’s own pages and its own COA documents. No retailer listing, no review site and no affiliate roundup was used as a source for any figure.

  • Part used comes from the Supplement Facts panel where one exists. On several brands that panel is an image rather than text, so the wording was read off the label image on the brand’s own page.
  • Extraction counts only where the brand states a method or a ratio. “Freeze-dried extract” is a drying step, not an extraction.
  • Published actives separates beta-glucan from polysaccharides on purpose. A polysaccharide total on a grain-grown product includes the starch from the grain, so the two are not comparable and are never merged into one cell.
  • COA access is Published only where a reader can open the document in one click. A testing claim in body copy, or a blog post about testing, is recorded as Not available.

Could not be checked

  • Naturealm / Sacred 7 (the brand site is a parked page and sacred7.com redirects away, so nothing the brand publishes can be read)
