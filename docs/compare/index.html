Lion's Mane and Reishi extracts, compared

Twenty brands checked against their own product pages and their own certificates of analysis. Twenty-six extracts scored, nine brands found to be selling something that is not an extract at all, and every figure traceable to the page it came from.

What is in scope. Concentrated extracts only: hot water, alcohol, or both. Milled mycelium grown on grain, plain mushroom powder, blends, coffees and gummies are not extracts, whatever the label says, so they are listed separately with the brand's own wording and the reason, rather than scored against products that are.

Five stars means all four of: fruiting body, a stated extraction method, a published active-compound figure, and a COA you can open in one click. Each one missing drops a star. A polysaccharide total is not a beta-glucan figure and does not earn that point, which is why two brands advertising 40% and 20% sit near the bottom.

Sold as extracts, but not extracts

Nine of the twenty brands checked do not sell a concentrated extract of either species. These are the products a shopper most often puts side by side with the tables above. Each one is quoted from the brand's own page, because in almost every case the brand says it plainly and the marketing says something else.

  • Host Defense — Mycelium grown on fermented brown rice, milled rather than extracted. Their own FAQ: “we don’t isolate or concentrate any particular compounds in our powdered products.” The “over 55% polysaccharides” guarantee is a polysaccharide total on a rice matrix, not a beta-glucan figure. Their liquid extracts do contain fruiting body, but as a proprietary blend with no ratio.
  • Om Mushrooms — Mycelial biomass and fruit body cultured on whole oats, with the oats included in the 2 g serving. Their own FAQ: “No. We don’t use hot water or alcohol extraction.”
  • Genius Mushrooms — Mycelium on oats, and extract-free by design: “Made with 100% extract-free, 100% organic mushrooms.”
  • Solaray — Fermented mycelium cultured on whole oats or brown rice. No extraction method and no ratio published.
  • Nutricost — Labelled mushroom powder, not an extract. No method, no ratio, no actives figure.
  • Swanson Full Spectrum — Their own page: “contains whole reishi mushroom rather than a concentrated extract.”
  • Double Wood Lion’s Mane — Their own answer on the product page: “This product is not an extract. Our Lions Mane is ground up Lions Mane.” The Reishi product is a 4:1 extract and is scored above.
  • Toniiq — Publishes the best single document in this whole survey, a one-click COA with a Megazyme beta-glucan assay, but the product page calls it whole mushroom powder and states no extraction method, so it cannot be scored as an extract.
  • Sun Potion — Their own spec table: “Full-spectrum mushroom powder (not an extract)”, despite publishing 52.2% beta-glucans on a recent Lion’s Mane lot.
  • Four Sigmatic — No single-species Lion’s Mane or Reishi extract. Every mushroom product is a blend, coffee, cocoa or elixir packet.
  • NOW Rei-Shi — A reishi and shiitake blend, not a single-species reishi extract. NOW’s Lion’s Mane is a single-species extract and is scored above.
  • Gummies, coffees and drink mixes — Excluded as a format across every brand in the survey.

How each cell was checked

One pass, 2026-11-11, against the brand's own pages and its own COA documents. No retailer listing, no review site, no affiliate roundup was used as a source for any figure, including where the brand's own site was slow or rate-limited.

  • Part used is taken from the Supplement Facts panel where one exists. On several brands that panel is an image rather than text, so the wording was read off the label image on the brand's own product page.
  • Extraction counts only if the brand states a method or a ratio. “Freeze-dried extract” is a drying step, not an extraction, and is recorded as such.
  • Published actives separates beta-glucan from polysaccharides on purpose. A polysaccharide total on a grain-grown product includes the starch from the grain, so the two numbers are not comparable and are never merged into one cell.
  • COA access is Published only where a reader can open the document in one click. “Third-party tested” in body copy, a blog post about testing, or a quality page with no document is recorded as Not available, because none of them can be checked.

What the survey found

  • The mass-market tier publishes nothing. Across eleven product pages from NOW, Swanson, Solaray, Nutricost and Micro Ingredients there is not one beta-glucan percentage and not one COA. Where a number appears it is a polysaccharide total.
  • The COA is the dividing line, not the mushroom. Several brands publish a percentage with no document, and one publishes an excellent document for a product that is not an extract at all.
  • In-house assays are normal at the premium end. One brand in the survey sends metals, microbials and pesticides to an outside lab but runs its own glucan assay. Only one has an outside lab behind the glucan number itself.
  • Brands contradict themselves. One page calls the same product dual-extracted and triple-extracted; another names two species that are not in the product; a third publishes two different extract weights for one capsule.
  • Species substitution is real. One Reishi in the table is Ganoderma tsugae, not G. lucidum, which the brand discloses and most shoppers will not notice.

Could not be checked

  • Naturealm / Sacred 7 (the brand site is a parked page and sacred7.com redirects away, so nothing the brand publishes can be read)
